Sorting

  • Insertion SortLecture 3. Take each key and shift the larger keys of the sorted part right. Shifts = inversions. Θ(n²) worst, Θ(n) best.
  • Bubble SortLecture 3. Compare neighbours, swap, stop early after a pass with no swap. Θ(n²) worst, Θ(n) best.
  • Counting SortLecture 3. Count, prefix sums, stable right-to-left placement. Θ(n + k).
  • Merge SortLecture 4. Split, sort halves, merge; call tree, call stack, bottom-up version and inversion counting. Θ(n log n).
  • Quick SortLecture 5. Lomuto and Hoare partition, fixed or random pivot, the worst case on sorted input, quickselect. Θ(n log n) expected.

Searching

  • Search Algorithms: Blind and HeuristicBFS, DFS, uniform-cost, depth-limited, iterative deepening and bidirectional search; greedy best-first, A* and hill climbing. Frontier, explored set, search tree, admissibility check and a side-by-side comparison on the same graph.

Graphs

  • Shortest PathsBFS, Dijkstra, Bellman–Ford, DAG shortest paths, Floyd–Warshall and Johnson. Relaxation, negative edges and negative cycles.
  • Minimum Spanning TreesKruskal with union-find, Prim, Borůvka and reverse-delete. Cut and cycle properties.

Strings and arrays

  • String MatchingNaive, Rabin–Karp, finite automaton, KMP, Boyer–Moore–Horspool and the Z algorithm.
  • Prefix and Suffix SumsRange queries, difference arrays, 2-D prefix sums, subarray sum = k, maximum subarray, trapping rain water.

Design techniques

  • Dynamic ProgrammingFibonacci by recursion, memoization and tabulation; rod cutting, coin change, LIS, 0/1 knapsack, LCS, edit distance and matrix-chain multiplication. Tables filled cell by cell, then traced back.
  • N-Queens ProblemBacktracking for the first and all solutions with the state-space tree, O(1) safety checks with column and diagonal arrays, and min-conflicts local search.

Hard problems and games

  • Travelling Salesman ProblemBrute force, Held–Karp dynamic programming and branch and bound with reduced cost matrices; nearest neighbour, the MST 2-approximation and 2-opt.
  • Game Theory AlgorithmsMinimax and alpha–beta pruning, saddle points, mixed strategies by the graphical method, iterated dominance, Nash equilibria and Nim.
